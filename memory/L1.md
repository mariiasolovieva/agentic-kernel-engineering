# L1 — Section-by-section summary

Source: `data/papers/shuaib2024_multikernel_amorphous.pdf`
Shuaib, Ori, Thomas, Masson, Bouzid. "Multikernel similarity-based clustering of amorphous systems and machine-learned interatomic potentials by active learning." J. Am. Ceram. Soc. 2025;108:e20128.

## Abstract
Presents a hybrid similarity kernel integrating short-range (SOAP) and long-range (Coulomb matrix) descriptors via an average-kernel approach. Combined with active-learning (AL) spectral clustering, it classifies amorphous configurations into uncorrelated clusters. A minimal database is built by sampling a small fraction of each cluster, and a Gaussian approximation potential (GAP) MLIP is fitted with Bayesian optimization (BO) of hyperparameters, embedded in an AL loop that grows the database when an energy-convergence threshold is not met. Tested on two amorphous systems from first-principles MD; accurate potentials with <2 meV/atom RMSE achieved with only 175 configurations. Robustness confirmed on models of several thousand atoms.

## Introduction
MLIPs (NN and kernel/GAP frameworks) extend simulation size/time beyond quantum-mechanical methods while keeping accuracy. For amorphous systems, FPMD is heavy and costly (up to a year of computing). MLIP accuracy depends on (i) database construction and (ii) model hyperparameters. The paper addresses both: (i) classification of snapshots by similarity to build minimal yet representative databases, and (ii) optimal hyperparameter selection. RMSD is inadequate for comparing structures where atoms cannot be mapped one-to-one; descriptors invariant to rotations/translations/permutations combined into a global similarity measure are the promising route.

## Computational methods (Section 2)
- **FPMD datasets**: three glassy systems — AsTe3 (240 atoms, 60As/180Te), TeO2 (480 atoms, 160Te/320O), and TeO2 + γ-TeO2 crystal (48 atoms). All BLYP DFT, energies/forces/stresses recalculated with a unique setup (1000 Ry cutoff).
- **SOAP descriptor**: local atomic environment density as sum of Gaussians (Eq. 1); expanded in spherical harmonics and radial basis (Eq. 2); power spectrum vector P(env); polynomial kernel k = [l·P·P' + c0]^d with d=4 (Eq. 3); distance d = sqrt(2−2k) (Eq. 4). Implemented in DScribe, lmax=nmax=8.
- **Coulomb matrix**: pairwise two-body descriptor (Eq. 5); Laplacian kernel w = exp[−ζ‖C−C'‖] (Eq. 6); zero-padding for consistent dimensions across varying atom counts. Kernel normalization (Eq. 7) gives self-similarity 1.

## Models / Methodology (Sections 2.3–2.5)
- **Hybrid similarity matrix**: pairwise SOAP similarity C_ij(A,B) (Eq. 8); average kernel K_SOAP(A,B) = 1/(N_A·N_B) Σ_ij C_ij = [l·(1/N_A Σ_i P(env_i^A))ᵀ·(1/N_B Σ_j P(env_j^B)) + c0]^d (Eq. 9) — computed cheaply by retaining the average SOAP fingerprint. Same for Coulomb (Eq. 10). Hybrid kernel K_hybrid = (1−δ)·K_SOAP + δ·K_Coulomb (Eq. 11); distance D = sqrt(2−2K_hybrid) (Eq. 12). δ varied 0→1 in steps of 0.1.
- **Spectral clustering**: distance matrix fed to spectral clustering (Scikit-learn); number of clusters chosen by convergence of average inter-cluster distance below d_tol; best δ chosen by lowest average energy standard deviation <σ>_E across clusters. MDS for 2D visualization.
- **Active learning MLIP fit**: sample fraction I_perc from each cluster; split 70/30 train/test; fit GAP (SOAP + two-body descriptor in QUIP); BO optimizes GAP hyperparameters on test RMSE; energy threshold E_tol = 2 meV/atom; if not met, grow database by I_perc per AL iteration until convergence or 100% of data.
- **ML modeling**: MLIP-MD in LAMMPS (NVT/NPT, Nosé–Hoover), annealing cycles; system sizes up to ~30 000 atoms.

## Problem statement
The core problem: how to build a minimal yet representative training database and fit an accurate, stable MLIP for amorphous systems, automating both data selection (via similarity-based clustering) and hyperparameter optimization (via BO), without manual trial-and-error.

## Results & Conclusion
Best δ = 0.6 (B), 0.7 (C), 0.3 (A). MLIP(0) (optimized δ) converged in 3–4 AL iterations with 97/127/175 configurations (10%/10%/14% of data), outperforming MLIP(1) (δ=0, SOAP-only) which needed more data and was unstable at large sizes. MLIP(0) matches MLIP(2) (full data) and FPMD/experiment in structure factors and PDFs (R_X ~7% for AsTe3, ~40–44% for TeO2). Conclusion: hybrid local+long-range descriptor with proper δ plus AL yields accurate, stable MLIPs from minimal databases, highlighting data-distillation potential.
## Results
- **Clustering**: δ varied 0→1 (step 0.1); best δ chosen by lowest average energy std-dev <σ>_E across clusters → 0.6 (B), 0.7 (C), 0.3 (A). For δ=0 (SOAP-only) clusters are widely dispersed with overlap across temperature plateaus and high <σ>_E; adding a small long-range fraction (δ<0.3) sharply reduces cluster count and yields well-organized temperature-dependent clusters. AsTe3 needed a tighter d_tol=10⁻⁴ due to high inter-configuration similarity.
- **AL MLIP**: MLIP(0) (optimized δ) converged in 3–4 AL iterations with 97/127/175 configurations (10%/10%/14% of data) and RMSE <2.0 meV/atom (forces <0.26 eV/Å); MLIP(1) (δ=0) needed more data (21%/7%) and was unstable at large sizes for AsTe3. MLIP(0) matches MLIP(2) (full ~1000-config data).
- **MLIP-MD**: MLIP(0) reproduces FPMD and experiment in total and partial Faber–Ziman structure factors and PDFs up to ~30 000 atoms, with only minor size effects. R_X (Wright parameter) ~7% for AsTe3, ~40–44% for TeO2; MLIP(1) shows higher R_X (51→62%) and overestimates peak intensities.
- **Coordination numbers**: MLIP(0) and MLIP(2) agree with FPMD (e.g. Te 3.70–3.75, O 1.85–1.87 for TeO2); MLIP(1) overestimates Te/O coordination and misrepresents l-fold environments, while MLIP(0) captures the Te–Te shoulder at ~4.3 Å and the ~10% increase in threefold Te in AsTe3.

## Discussion and Conclusion
The hybrid local (SOAP) + long-range (Coulomb) similarity kernel with a properly tuned weight δ, embedded in an AL loop with BO-optimized GAP hyperparameters, yields accurate and stable MLIPs from minimal databases (<200 configurations) with ab initio accuracy. Models built on clusters from SOAP-only similarity (δ=0) are less accurate and, for AsTe3, unstable beyond 240 atoms, showing that accounting for long-range correlations is essential for capturing network connectivity in amorphous systems. The minimal-database MLIP(0) reproduces the results of a full-data MLIP(2), highlighting the data-distillation potential of the approach. The workflow is general and readily applicable to other disordered systems.
